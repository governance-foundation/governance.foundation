## Why this order works better

- `Why Governance Foundation?` works as the anchor statement of intent.
- `A case for Governance Framework` naturally follows by explaining why governance cannot just be copied as a finished pattern.
- `Why Frameworks Fail?` then sharpens that critique by showing how frameworks collide with living organisations.
- `Governance Experience Platform` lands better after those critiques because it reads as a practical answer, not just another abstract model.
- `Documentation is not knowledge` then narrows the focus from governance structure into the knowledge problem.
- `Knowledge Management 4.0` works best last because it feels like the forward-looking bridge into KnowledgeFund and AI-legible organisational memory.

## Date order vs conceptual order

The current dates are acceptable for backfill, but the best reading flow is not exactly the same as the current chronology.

Current chronology places `Governance Experience Platform` before `Why Frameworks Fail?` and places `Knowledge Management 4.0` before `Documentation is not knowledge`.

Conceptually, the cleaner order is:

- critique copy-paste governance first
- critique framework absorption next
- then introduce GXP as the operational answer
- explain the documentation/knowledge distinction before moving into KM 4.0 as the broader system answer

This does not require changing file dates unless the blog chronology itself matters more than conceptual progression.

## Main overlap review

### Healthy overlap

Some repetition is useful because the pieces are part of one worldview.
The following recurring themes are healthy and should remain:

- governance is more than oversight
- organisations are living socio-technical systems
- fragmentation is expensive
- shared structure matters
- AI raises the cost of incoherence

These act as the through-line across the set.

### Overlap to watch

#### 1. Oversight/reactive governance
Appears in:
- `Why Governance Foundation?`
- `A case for Governance Framework`
- `Governance Experience Platform`

This is mostly fine, but each article should do a distinct job:
- `Why Governance Foundation?` = define governance more broadly
- `A case for Governance Framework` = explain why reactive/copy-paste governance does not scale
- `Governance Experience Platform` = explain how governance becomes usable upstream through lived experience

**Recommendation:** keep all three, but avoid adding any more generic anti-oversight paragraphs.

#### 2. Fragmentation / trapped knowledge
Appears in:
- `Documentation is not knowledge`
- `Knowledge Management 4.0`
- lightly in `Governance Experience Platform`

This is also mostly healthy, but the distinction should stay crisp:
- `Documentation is not knowledge` = diagnosis of the documentation mistake
- `Knowledge Management 4.0` = systemic answer for living organisational memory
- `GXP` = broader governance/experience coherence layer, not primarily a docs/KM essay

**Recommendation:** if future edits happen, keep `Documentation is not knowledge` narrower and avoid letting it drift too far into KM-system design.

#### 3. AI-legibility
Appears in:
- `Governance Experience Platform`
- `Why Frameworks Fail?`
- `Knowledge Management 4.0`

This works, but AI should stay a secondary sharpening device in the older pieces, not the main subject.

**Recommendation:** keep AI references short in the backfill set and let the dedicated 2026 articles carry the heavier AI argument.
