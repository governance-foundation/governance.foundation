## Article plans and key points

### 1. Why Governance Foundation?
**Action:** keep, light polish  
**Goal:** establish the site as a place for foundational governance thinking, not compliance theatre.  
**Audience:** governance-curious readers, transformation leaders, thoughtful generalists.

**Key points**
- governance is not just oversight, committees, or compliance gates
- governance is the living structure that shapes behaviour, decision-making, coordination, and adaptation
- frameworks applied without a foundation become theatre
- Governance Foundation exists to develop the underlying concepts, distinctions, and models first
- governance should be treated as a living foundation, not an after-the-fact control layer

**Likely polish tasks**
- tighten prose
- remove repetitive framing
- align terminology with newer writing on living systems and knowledge flow

---

### 2. A case for Governance Framework
**Action:** rewrite  
**Goal:** explain why governance patterns cannot be copied blindly between organisations.  
**Audience:** transformation leads, consultants, governance practitioners.

**Key points**
- copy-paste governance does not work because governance patterns are shaped by organisational history and maturity
- every organisation is a socio-technical system with its own accumulated structure, incentives, and scars
- governance should grow from the organisation’s actual conditions, not be imported as a finished template
- a useful governance foundation acts more like scaffolding than a rigid model
- the right aim is guided evolution, not pattern transplantation

**Suggested modernization angle**
- shift from generic anti-copying argument toward a clearer critique of governance portability
- connect strongly to organisational maturity, knowledge structure, and adaptation

**Possible retitle**
- `Why governance patterns do not transfer cleanly`
- `Why copy-paste governance fails`

---

### 3. Governance Experience Platform
**Action:** rewrite hard  
**Goal:** explain GXP as a model for shared organisational experiences built on common knowledge, not just a platform label.  
**Audience:** systems thinkers, product/governance architects, innovation and ops leaders.

**Key points**
- organisations fail when shared purpose and shared understanding fragment across teams
- communication, collaboration, cooperation, and coordination break down when people do not share usable context
- a governance experience platform should create tailored local experiences without losing common organisational structure
- the point is not centralised control, but higher coherence through shared knowledge and better translation into action
- AI becomes useful here only when it sits on top of a legible, connected knowledge body
- GXP should be framed as enabling organisational listening, synchronisation, and experience design

**Suggested modernization angle**
- reduce abstract platform talk
- make the article more concrete about what experiences are and why they matter
- connect GXP to current KnowledgeFund and AI-legibility ideas

**Possible retitle**
- `What a Governance Experience Platform is really for`
- `Why organisations need shared governance experiences`

---

### 4. Why Frameworks Fail?
**Action:** rewrite  
**Goal:** explain why frameworks fail socially and structurally even when they look sound on paper.  
**Audience:** consultants, architects, transformation leaders, framework creators.

**Key points**
- frameworks are compressed knowledge icebergs, not magic uploads
- organisations are also knowledge icebergs with their own history, habits, incentives, and internal contradictions
- failure often comes from misalignment between the framework and the organisation, not just poor training or tooling
- social rejection matters more than many framework advocates admit
- passive resistance, local incentives, and translation burdens erode framework adoption over time
- information governance becomes a side-hustle when the organisation lacks shared structures and incentives
- framework success depends on alignment, integration, and organisational evolution, not template compliance

**Suggested modernization angle**
- make the iceberg metaphor sharper and more legible
- connect framework failure to knowledge fragmentation and AI-era context
- reduce older awkward phrasing and repetition

**Possible retitle**
- `Why frameworks fail in living organisations`
- `Frameworks fail when organisations cannot absorb them`

---

### 5. Knowledge Management 4.0
**Action:** rebuild from scratch  
**Goal:** turn this into the true bridge from traditional KM to KnowledgeFund.  
**Audience:** knowledge management leaders, strategy leaders, AI/gov readers, organisational designers.

**Key points**
- traditional knowledge management is heavily translation-based and prone to fragmentation and staleness
- a lot of organisational knowledge remains person-bound, hidden, or trapped in disconnected systems
- documentation and repositories do not automatically create shared usable knowledge
- AI changes the economics of interpretation, connection, extraction, and maintenance
- the next stage is not more document storage, but AI-assisted organisational knowledge formation
- KnowledgeFund should be introduced as a more living, traceable, connected, reusable organisational knowledge system
- the aim is to move from static records toward active organisational memory and learning

**Suggested modernization angle**
- make this the explicit transition piece from classic KM into KnowledgeFund
- connect to ontology, traceability, working knowledge, and organisational memory
- keep it readable, not jargon-heavy

**Possible retitle**
- `From knowledge management to KnowledgeFund`
- `Why knowledge management needs a new operating model`

---

### 6. Documentation is not knowledge
**Action:** keep, light polish  
**Goal:** distinguish documentation from living organisational knowledge.  
**Audience:** broad, especially anyone working with docs, process, transformation, or AI.

**Key points**
- documentation is a record, not the whole knowledge reality
- a document can exist while the organisation still lacks usable shared knowledge
- fragmentation, drift, and disconnected records create document-rich but knowledge-poor systems
- documentation usually captures only a narrow formal subset of what people actually know
- much of the valuable working knowledge includes rationale, failures, constraints, heuristics, and warnings
- a knowledge page is not the same thing as documentation
- organisations need connected, interpretable, maintained knowledge, not just more files

**Likely polish tasks**
- tighten a few paragraphs
- ensure it lines up cleanly with the rebuilt `Knowledge Management 4.0`
