## 1) Why Governance Foundation?

Most organisations still treat governance as if it begins where things go wrong.

Oversight.
Compliance.
Escalation.
Approval.

But governance is much bigger than that.
It is the structure through which an organisation creates coherence, direction, and usable shared reality.

If governance only appears after fragmentation, drift, and misalignment have already taken hold, it is arriving too late.

That is the starting point behind Governance Foundation.

**Full article:** `docusaurus/blog/2014-06-16-governance-foundation.md`

## 2) Why Frameworks Fail

Frameworks usually do not fail because they are empty.
They fail because they meet organisations that are alive.

A framework can be internally elegant and still collapse on contact with local history, informal workarounds, team incentives, inherited systems, and person-bound knowledge.

The problem is not just “bad adoption”.
The problem is assuming a static structure can simply be dropped into a living socio-technical system.

That is why so many organisations keep buying alignment and getting friction instead.

**Full article:** `docusaurus/blog/2014-10-02-why-frameworks-fail.md`

## 3) From Local Workflow to Shared Ontology

Most organisations do not have one clean model of how they work.
They have many local workflows, each optimized for a team, a tool, or a task.

That works — until the organisation needs reuse.
Or coordination.
Or traceability.
Or AI.

Then the cost of local optimisation shows up as semantic fragmentation.
Everyone is talking about roughly the same reality, but through incompatible structures.

This is where the move from workflow to ontology starts to matter.

**Full article:** `docusaurus/blog/2015-04-21-from-local-workflow-to-shared-ontology.md`

## 4) Documentation Is Not Knowledge

A lot of organisations still confuse having documents with having knowledge.

But documentation usually captures only the thin formal slice of what people need to know.
The most important context often remains in heads, habits, side conversations, spreadsheets, workarounds, and “the way things actually happen here”.

That is why documentation-heavy organisations can still be deeply knowledge-poor.

The problem is not just missing pages.
It is the absence of living, reusable organisational memory.

**Full article:** `docusaurus/blog/2015-05-12-documentation-is-not-knowledge.md`

---

## 5) What a Shared Organisational Model Is Actually For

A shared organisational model is not there to make the enterprise look tidy.

It is not diagram theatre.
It is not architecture wallpaper.
It is not a prestige artifact for governance teams.

Its real purpose is to let meaning travel.
Across teams.
Across tools.
Across planning, delivery, operations, and change.

Without that, organisations keep recreating the same knowledge in isolated forms and paying the integration cost over and over again.

**Full article:** `docusaurus/blog/2015-06-24-what-a-shared-organisational-model-is-actually-for.md`
