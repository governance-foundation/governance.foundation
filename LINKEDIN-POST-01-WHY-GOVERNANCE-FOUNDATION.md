## Small summary notes

### Article direction

This article should feel like:

- a **reset**
- a **thesis statement**
- a **reframing of governance**

It should not get lost in tooling, AI, or detailed ontology yet.
Its job is to establish the core claim:

> governance is a structural capability for coherence, not just an oversight mechanism.

### How it ties to the next article

This naturally leads into:

- **A Case for Governance Foundation**
- then **Why Frameworks Fail**

Why:

- once you redefine governance more broadly, the obvious next question is:
  **why do so many governance structures still fail in practice?**
- that opens the path into framework failure, local optimisation, fragmented knowledge, and eventually KnowledgeFund.

### How it ties to the future narrative

Later articles will progressively show:

1. governance failure is really a **coherence failure**
2. coherence failure is often a **knowledge structure failure**
3. knowledge structure failure becomes dangerous in the **AI era**
4. therefore organisations need **shared semantic structure / KnowledgeFund / ontology-backed collaboration**

So this first post is the **root claim** the rest of the narrative grows from.
